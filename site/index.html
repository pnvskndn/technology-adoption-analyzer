Principal findings

Momentum, not money, separates the leaders

Three results hold across the panel. Together they suggest that transition outcomes are governed more by the state a system is already in than by the economic conditions surrounding it.

01

Prior adoption dominates

A country's renewable share in the previous year predicts its current share far more strongly than income, urbanisation or education. Deployment behaves as momentum.

ImplicationIncentives at the margin do little in systems that have not started. What compounds is early capacity.
02

Wealth does not predict adoption

Grouping countries by adoption profile separates high-income renewable leaders from high-income fossil-incumbent economies at comparable income levels.

ImplicationScreening markets by income class points at the wrong countries. Energy mix and history matter more.
03

The gap maps the headroom

Comparing realised adoption against the level a country's fundamentals support reveals wide variation — some far above expectation, others far below.

ImplicationA large shortfall is quantified, addressable potential rather than a structural ceiling.
Signature measure

The structural gap

A second model is trained on structural fundamentals alone — income, growth, urbanisation, education, electricity consumption and incumbent fossil share — with every history-dependent term removed. It estimates the adoption level a country with those characteristics typically reaches. The difference from what the country actually achieves is the structural gap.

Percentage points of renewable electricity share. Positive values indicate a country outperforming what its structural conditions would suggest; negative values indicate unrealised potential. Associational benchmark, not a causal estimate.

Methodology

Four models over one panel

Annual World Bank indicators, cleaned and imputed, with a strict temporal split — earlier years for training, later years held out — so that no model is evaluated on data preceding what it learned from.

01

Per-country attribution

Rather than estimating one average effect per variable across all countries, the model's prediction is decomposed for each country individually, so the drivers identified for India can differ from those for Norway.

Gradient boosting · SHAP values
02

Country forecasts

A five-year projection of renewable share for every country in the panel, each with an explicit uncertainty interval rather than a single point estimate.

Prophet · 80% intervals
03

Adoption archetypes

Countries are grouped by their energy and economic profile rather than by region or income band, surfacing peer sets that conventional classifications obscure.

K-means · PCA projection
04

Structural benchmark

A model deliberately blind to each country's own history, estimating the adoption its fundamentals support. Its residual is the structural gap — the quantity of interest rather than error to minimise.

Gradient boosting · lag terms excluded
Validation

Measured accuracy, not claimed precision

Rather than reporting how closely the models fit the data they were trained on, forecasts are tested against history. The models are trained only on data up to a cut-off year, asked to project the years that followed, and compared against what actually occurred — repeated across successive cut-offs.

Every projection carries its own track record

For each country the platform reports how far its forecasts have historically missed, and how often reality fell inside the stated uncertainty range. A forecast without an error history is an assertion; with one, it is evidence.

What this analysis does not establish

Association, not causation

The models identify which conditions accompany higher adoption. They do not establish that changing a variable would produce a given change in outcome.

A partial measure of transition

Renewable share of electricity excludes heat and transport, and counts a large hydropower endowment the same as actively deployed wind and solar — conflating geography with policy effort.

No institutional variables

The dataset contains no measure of regulatory quality, market structure or policy stringency, which are likely to matter considerably. The structural gap currently absorbs them.

Sparse annual data

Roughly two decades of annual observations per country, with World Bank indicators lagging reality by about two years. Small by the standards of the model class used.

In development

Measuring policy where no index reaches

Established policy stringency indices cover mainly high-income economies — excluding much of the Gulf, South and Southeast Asia and Africa, which are largely the same countries showing the widest structural gaps. The next phase constructs that missing measure from policy text.

01

Assemble

Structured policy databases, supplemented by national energy strategy documents for countries they cover thinly.

02

Classify

Natural language processing over policy text, scoring instruments by type, scope and bindingness.

03

Validate

Correlate the derived score against an established index across the countries where both exist.

04

Test

Add the measure to the model and ask whether policy absence explains the structural gap.
